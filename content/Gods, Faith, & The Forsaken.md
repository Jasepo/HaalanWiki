- **The Origin of Faith:** According to ancient traditions, Gods once lived physically in the Mortal World. Mortals eventually rejected the Gods' commands to fight their divine wars, made peace, and built civilizations. Those who chose worship were cast into the [[The Umberdeep & Underground|underground]].

- **Clerics in Haalan:** Clerics do not need to worship a god. Divine magic comes from absolute conviction in a belief, teaching, community, philosophy, or spiritual tradition (faith itself). Congregations are communities built around shared teachings rather than traditional temples.
#### Modern View on Religion & Clerical Power

- **Secular Shift:** Coinciding with the founding of [[Haalan]] 275 years ago, society largely abandoned worship, believing that the old gods' influence only incited endless war. Modern [[Locations/Haalan|Haalani]] culture prioritizes technology, cooperation, and knowledge over religion, treating the old deities as ancient mythology.
- **Internal Faith:** Clerical power does not depend on external divine entities; magic stems entirely from internal conviction and personal faith.

#### The Original Six Gods & Statues

- **[[Varkos]]:** An enormous, heavily muscled barbarian with a wild mane of dark hair and a long, unkempt beard. He wore weathered furs and battle-scarred armor, carrying a massive ax. His expression was stern and worried; rather than bloodthirsty, his eyes reflected the terrible cost of war. God of Death and War, associated with [[Borgan]].
  
- **[[Solara]]:** A tall, regal woman, possibly an Aasimar, with bronze skin and flowing golden hair that shined like sunlight. She wore ivory robes embroidered with gold and a crown resembling sun rays, her face beautiful but commanding, embodying both the sun's loving warmth and its merciless heat. Sun Goddess, associated with [[Luvel]].
  
- **[[Selvara]]:** A slender, pale elven woman with long silver hair and luminous, almost colorless eyes. She wore flowing, midnight-blue garments adorned with stars and delicate crescent moon edges, her face partially obscured by a translucent veil. She watches over the mysteries of the night. Moon Goddess, associated with [[Izedel]].
  
- **[[Ravel]]:** A tall, thin figure of undisclosed age, with one youthful hand and one aged hand. Their face is concealed beneath a deep hood, a delicate thread of silver and red stretched between their fingers, symbolizing the countless possible paths of mortal lives. Trickster God, associated with [[Mivolan]].
  
- **[[Odrun]]:** An elderly, broad-shouldered warrior with a magnificent braided gray beard, thick arms, and hands blackened by centuries of smithing. He wore a leather apron over practical garments, with a smith's hammer in one hand and an open book in the other. His expression is thoughtful and patient. God of Knowledge, associated with [[Drabasha]].
  
- **[[Thaelwyn]]:** A towering sea elf figure, possessing both masculine and feminine features in perfect harmony. Their tall, powerful physique contrasts with their delicate facial features, flowing hair resembling tangled seaweed, and a crown of branching coral and antlers. Their skin carried the shifting colors of forests and oceans, while their garments appeared woven from leaves, kelp, and driftwood. Neither a man nor a woman, Thaelwyn embodies the duality of nature, representing the fluid balance between creation and destruction, tranquility and ferocity, land and sea. God of Nature, associated with [[Regalla]].

#### The Three Forsaken (Forbidden Cults)

- **[[Azrathul]] (The King Beneath):** Embodies absolute control, domination, and the belief that freedom is a mistake. Followers believe peace requires unquestioning obedience to a single ruler, attracting tyrants and conquerors. Depicted as a massive orc king in black iron armor with a prison-bar crown, hidden face, and an immense scepter of absolute dominion. Ancient carvings show him seated above kneeling subjects.

- **[[Velshara]] (The Mother of Worms):** Embodies decay, consumption, and inevitable corruption. Her followers teach that death and decomposition are the only truths, viewing resistance to disease or decay as a violation of fundamental order. Her forbidden worship has grown amid recent suffering. Visually described with long, thin fingers, rotten burial garments, and worms crawling through her hair and skin, yet bearing a strangely tender, maternal expression.

- **[[Nhal]] (The Empty God):** The most poorly understood and least known of the Forsaken, accounts conflict, and much of its history was deliberately erased following the god's defeat. Sculptures depict a robed humanoid figure with a deliberately damaged face. Scholars suspect it was added later and is not an original deity.

#### The Seventh God Legend

- **Disputed Origins:** Ancient texts conflict on whether there were originally six or seven gods. Legends claim a seventh god, debated as either Azrathul or Velshara. created the disruption and sparked the ancient wars. Due to contradictory historical accounts, modern society largely views these divine narratives as old mythology.
